Life

A boy from the shoreline who reached for the sky

Born on 15 October 1931 in the temple town of Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Kalam grew up in a family of modest means — his father owned a small boat, his mother ran the household — and sold newspapers as a boy to contribute to the family income. What he lacked in privilege, he made up for in an unshakable curiosity about flight and the stars.

Early Life

A student of physics, then flight

He studied physics at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, before training in aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology — a decision that set the direction for the rest of his working life.

Career

From satellites to missiles

He joined India's defence research establishment and later its space programme, helping build the SLV-III rocket that placed the Rohini satellite in orbit. He went on to lead the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, shaping the Agni and Prithvi missiles and earning the name "Missile Man of India."

Public Service

The people's President

In 2002 he was elected the 11th President of India, a role in which his warmth, humility, and habit of speaking directly to students earned him the affectionate title "the People's President."

Legacy

A teacher above all titles

After his presidency he returned to what he loved most — teaching. He continued lecturing, writing, and mentoring young minds until his final day, leaving behind a legacy less about rank and more about relentless curiosity and service.

Flight Path

A trajectory, not a straight line

Every life has a launch, a climb, and a course correction. Here is the arc of his — from a small-town classroom to the frontier of India's space and missile programmes, and finally to the highest office in the land.

  1. 1931

    Born in Rameswaram

    Born into a family of modest means on the island town of Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu — a childhood shaped by the sea, curiosity, and quiet hardship.

  2. 1958

    Graduates in aerospace engineering

    Completes his aerospace engineering studies at the Madras Institute of Technology and joins India's aeronautical research establishment soon after.

  3. 1969

    Moves to India's space programme

    Transfers to the newly forming Indian Space Research Organisation, taking on project leadership for the country's first satellite launch vehicle.

  4. 1980

    SLV-III reaches orbit

    The SLV-III rocket, under his project direction, successfully places the Rohini satellite into orbit — a defining early milestone for Indian spaceflight.

  5. 1980s–90s

    Leads the missile programme

    Heads the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, steering the development of the Agni and Prithvi missiles and earning the title "Missile Man of India."

  6. 1997

    Awarded the Bharat Ratna

    Receives India's highest civilian honour in recognition of his contributions to science and the nation.

  7. 1998

    Chief Scientific Adviser

    Serves as Chief Scientific Adviser to the Government of India during the Pokhran-II nuclear tests, a period that placed him at the centre of national attention.

  8. 2002

    Elected 11th President of India

    Takes office as President, quickly becoming known for visiting schools, answering students' letters personally, and speaking plainly about ambition and effort.

  9. 2007

    Returns to teaching and writing

    Completes his presidential term and returns to the classroom and the page, authoring books and lecturing at institutions across the country.

  10. 2015

    Passes away mid-lecture

    On 27 July 2015, he collapses and passes away while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong — teaching, fittingly, until his very last moment.
